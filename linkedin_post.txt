Most AI agents are stateless. They forget everything the moment the conversation ends.

That's fine for a chatbot. It's a real problem when you're debugging a production outage at 2 AM and the agent has no idea your team fixed the same Redis OOM six months ago.

I built DevOpsGuard — an incident response agent that remembers every post-mortem your team has ever written, and surfaces the exact fix when a similar error fires again.

Here's what changes when you add Hindsight agent memory:

Without memory → "Increase maxmemory, check your eviction policy" (generic, useless at 2 AM)

With memory → "This looks like INC-101 from September. You fixed it by changing maxmemory-policy to volatile-lru and scaling to 4GB. The fix may have been reverted." (specific, actionable, grounded in your history)

The technical setup:
→ Hindsight retain() ingests post-mortems as free-form text — no schema needed
→ Hindsight recall() runs 4 retrieval strategies in parallel (semantic, BM25, graph, temporal)
→ Recalled memories inject directly into a Groq prompt for structured diagnosis
→ Hindsight reflect() synthesises cross-incident patterns without an extra LLM call

The thing that surprised me most: after ingesting just 5 post-mortems, recall returned 33 structured facts. Hindsight doesn't store text — it extracts entities, relationships, and facts. That's why the retrieval quality is so different from a naive vector search.

The before/after contrast is real and immediate. The memory layer is what makes the difference.
